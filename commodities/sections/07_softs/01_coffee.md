# Coffee

Two distinct species with separate futures: **Arabica** (higher quality, ~55–60% of output) and **Robusta** (hardier, higher caffeine, used in instant coffee and blends). Global production ~175 million 60-kg bags.

## Supply

| Origin | Type | Calendar / notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Brazil (~35–40%)** | Arabica + Robusta (conilon) | Harvest May–Sep; **frost risk Jun–Aug** (Minas Gerais); flowering Sep–Oct needs rain; Arabica on-/off-year biennial cycle |
| **Vietnam (~17%)** | Robusta | Harvest Oct–Jan; Central Highlands drought risk |
| Colombia | Washed Arabica | Two harvests; La Niña rains cut output |
| Indonesia | Robusta (Sumatra, Lampung) | Harvest Apr–Jul |
| Ethiopia, Honduras, Uganda, India, Peru, Central America | various | |

- Arabica trees take ~3–4 years to mature → slow supply response.
- **ICE certified stocks** (graded, exchange-deliverable) are a closely watched tightness indicator.

## Demand

- ~170+ Mbags/yr; EU, US, Brazil (big domestic consumer), Japan; fastest growth in Asia (China, Indonesia).
- Demand is fairly price-inelastic at retail; roasters change blends (more robusta) when arabica is expensive.
- **EUDR** (EU deforestation regulation) compliance costs and timing can create two-tier pricing.

## Price drivers

1. Brazilian weather (frost, drought) and crop estimates (CONAB, USDA attaché, private analysts).
2. **BRL**: weaker real encourages Brazilian farmer selling in USD terms (bearish).
3. Vietnamese weather and farmer hoarding.
4. ICE certified stocks; freight/container disruptions.
5. US tariffs on origin countries (2025 tariffs on Brazil caused trade-flow and price dislocations).
6. Speculative positioning; the 2024–2025 rally took arabica to record highs on consecutive Brazil/Vietnam shortfalls.

## Trading notes

- Differentials (physical premium over futures for a specific origin) matter as much as futures for physical traders.
- Backwardation signals tight nearby availability; certified stock trends confirm.
- Options skew is call-heavy into the Brazilian winter.
