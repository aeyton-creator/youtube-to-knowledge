# Cocoa

The most concentrated soft: **Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana produce ~55–60% of world cocoa**. A structural shock (disease, weather, underinvestment) took prices to record highs above $12,000/t in 2024 before demand destruction set in.

## Supply

- West Africa ~70%: Côte d'Ivoire (~2 Mt in normal years), Ghana, Cameroon, Nigeria; plus Ecuador (fast-growing), Brazil, Indonesia.
- **Main crop** Oct–Mar; **mid crop** Apr–Sep (smaller).
- Structural problems: **swollen shoot virus**, aging trees, illegal gold mining (galamsey) in Ghana, low farmgate prices historically, black pod disease in wet seasons, Harmattan dry winds (Dec–Feb).
- Government marketing boards fix farmgate prices and **forward-sell** crops (CCC in Côte d'Ivoire, COCOBOD in Ghana). Their forward sales and any defaults on contracts are important signals.
- **Living Income Differential (LID):** $400/t premium on Ivorian/Ghanaian cocoa since 2020.

## Demand

- Grindings (processing beans into butter, powder and liquor) are the demand proxy: quarterly **European (ECA), North American (NCA), Asian grindings** data.
- Price-elastic at very high prices: manufacturers reformulate (less cocoa content, smaller sizes).
- Grinders: Barry Callebaut, Cargill, Olam (ofi); chocolate: Mars, Mondelez, Hershey, Nestlé, Ferrero.

## Price drivers

1. Weekly **Ivorian port arrivals** vs last year.
2. West African weather: rainfall, soil moisture, Harmattan intensity.
3. Quarterly grind data.
4. ICE certified stocks (multi-decade lows in 2024).
5. ICCO balance estimates.
6. Farmgate price decisions and smuggling flows between countries.

## Trading notes

- Liquidity fell during 2024 as margin requirements surged; this amplified volatility and moved hedging to OTC.
- Bean quality (bean count, moisture) affects certification eligibility.
