# Gold

Gold trades less like a consumable commodity and more like a monetary asset. Above-ground stocks (~215,000 t) dwarf annual mine supply (~3,600 t), so flows of investment and central-bank demand dominate price formation.

## Supply

- Mine supply ~3,600 t/yr; China, Russia, Australia, Canada, US, Ghana are top producers. Mine supply is price-inelastic in the short run.
- Recycling ~1,100–1,400 t/yr and is the most price-elastic supply component (jewellery scrap surges on price spikes).
- All-in sustaining cost (AISC) for the industry gives a long-term cost reference, well below spot in recent years.

## Demand

| Segment | Driver |
|---|---|
| Jewellery (~45% of fabrication) | India and China; price-elastic; Indian wedding season and Diwali, Chinese New Year |
| Bar and coin | Retail investment; strong in Asia and Germany |
| ETFs | Western institutional flows; sensitive to real yields |
| Central banks | Reserve diversification away from USD since 2022 sanctions on Russia; >1,000 t/yr in 2022–2024 (PBoC, Poland, Turkey, India, etc.) |
| Technology | ~7%; electronics |

## Price drivers

1. **US real interest rates** (10-year TIPS yield): historically the strongest inverse correlation. This relationship weakened from 2022 as central-bank and Asian buying dominated.
2. **US dollar:** generally inverse.
3. **Geopolitical risk / sanctions / de-dollarisation** — central bank buying.
4. Fiscal deficits, debt sustainability concerns and inflation expectations.
5. Fed policy path; gold tends to rally into cutting cycles.
6. Physical premiums in India/China and ETF flows as confirmation signals.

## Market structure

- London OTC market is the hub; COMEX futures are the price-discovery venue in the US session.
- **EFP (COMEX vs London)** normally reflects financing and is small; in early 2025, tariff fears caused a large EFP blowout and massive metal flows from London to New York vaults.
- **Gold lease rates** (GOFO historically) show physical tightness.
- **Gold/silver ratio**, **gold/oil ratio** and **gold in other currencies** are standard cross-checks.

## Trading notes

- Watch the 10-year TIPS yield, DXY, central bank purchase data (World Gold Council monthly), Swiss and Hong Kong customs export data to China/India.
- Gold often sells off in liquidity crises (margin calls) before rallying.
- Implied vol is typically low relative to other commodities; skew is call-skewed in strong bull markets.
