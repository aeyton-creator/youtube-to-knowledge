## The cattle cycle

- Beef cattle have a long biological lag: cow bred → calf born ~9 months later → weaned at 6–8 months → backgrounded → **feedlot** ~150–180 days → slaughter at ~18–22 months. Heifer retention to rebuild the herd takes ~2 years to add supply.
- **The cycle runs ~8–12 years.** Drought forces liquidation (more beef short term, less later). The US herd fell to its lowest level since the early 1950s in 2024–2025, driving record cattle prices.
- **Heifer retention** paradoxically tightens supply further in the short term, since heifers are withheld from slaughter.

## Margins and spreads

- **Cattle crush / feeding margin:** live cattle − feeder cattle − feed (corn) cost. Feeder cattle price is inversely related to corn (feed cost).
- **Boxed beef cutout** (Choice/Select, USDA daily) vs cash fed cattle → packer margin. Packers (Tyson, JBS, Cargill, National Beef) cut slaughter when margins are negative.
- Live cattle futures normally trade at a discount/premium to the 5-area cash fed steer price; basis converges at expiry.

## Key reports

- **Cattle on Feed** (monthly): placements, marketings, on-feed numbers.
- Semi-annual **Cattle inventory** (January, July).
- Weekly slaughter, carcass weights, boxed beef, export sales.
- Drought monitor (pasture conditions).

## Other drivers

- Imports: Brazil, Australia; Mexican feeder imports (suspended in 2024–2025 due to **New World screwworm**).
- Tariffs on Brazilian beef; retail beef demand and consumer spending.
- Seasonality: grilling season demand (spring) supports cutout; fed cattle supplies tend to rise in summer.
