## Supply

- Mined supply ~23 Mt/yr copper contained; refined ~27–28 Mt including secondary (scrap).
- Leading producers: **Chile** (~23–25%), **DRC** (fast-growing, now #2), **Peru**, **China**, US, Russia, Indonesia, Australia, Zambia.
- **China** is ~45–50% of refined (smelting) output: it imports concentrate and refines.
- **Treatment and refining charges (TC/RCs):** fees smelters charge miners. Low or negative TCs (as in 2024–2025) mean concentrate is scarce relative to smelting capacity — a mine supply tightness signal.
- Long lead times: a new mine takes 10–15+ years from discovery to production; grades are declining (Chile).
- Disruption allowance: analysts assume 4–6% of mine supply is lost annually to strikes, accidents, weather, political events (e.g., Cobre Panamá closure 2023, Grasberg mudslide 2025, Kamoa-Kakula seismic event 2025).

## Demand

| Sector | Share (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Construction (wiring, plumbing) | ~28% |
| Electrical grid / power | ~28% |
| Consumer & industrial equipment | ~20% |
| Transport (incl. EVs — ~80 kg per BEV vs ~20 kg ICE) | ~12% |

- **China consumes ~55% of refined copper.** Grid investment (State Grid), property, and manufacturing exports matter.
- Energy-transition and AI data-centre demand provide the structural bull case; property weakness in China the bear case.

## Price drivers

1. Chinese demand: PMIs, credit (TSF), property, State Grid spend, Yangshan premium, SHFE stocks.
2. Mine disruptions and TC/RC levels.
3. USD and global growth expectations.
4. Inventory location across LME/COMEX/SHFE/bonded.
5. **US tariff policy** — the COMEX–LME arbitrage blew out in 2025 on Section 232 tariff expectations, pulling large stocks into the US.
6. Scrap availability (price-elastic supply).

## Trading notes

- Monitor COMEX–LME spread (tariff/arbitrage), LME cash-3s, cancelled warrants, Chinese import arbitrage and Yangshan premium.
- Copper/gold ratio tracks US 10-year yields and growth sentiment.
- Seasonality: Chinese demand strong in Q2 ("golden March/April") and soft around Lunar New Year.
