# The LME & Base Metals Market Structure

Most base metals price off the **London Metal Exchange (LME)**, with SHFE (Shanghai) and COMEX (copper) as important secondary venues.

## LME mechanics

- **Prompt date structure:** daily prompts out to 3 months, weekly to 6 months, then monthly out to 123 months for some metals. The **3-month (3M)** is the liquid benchmark.
- **Cash–3s spread:** the key tightness indicator. Backwardation = prompt scarcity; contango = surplus.
- **Tom/next** and **cash/3s** spreads can spike when one entity holds dominant positions; the LME's **lending rules** cap backwardations for dominant holders.
- **Official prices** set in the second ring session (cash settlement for many physical contracts); **closing prices** at 17:00 London.
- Trading venues: Ring (open outcry, the only one remaining in Europe), LMEselect (electronic), inter-office telephone.
- Monthly average contracts (TAPOs/TAS) are used by physical traders who price on monthly averages.

## Warehouses and stocks

- LME warrants are titles to metal in approved warehouses. **On-warrant** vs **cancelled warrants** (earmarked for withdrawal). Rising cancellations signal physical drawdown and often precede backwardations.
- Off-warrant stocks (reported monthly) and bonded Chinese stocks add a hidden layer.
- Since April 2024, Russian metal produced after the US/UK sanctions date cannot be delivered onto the LME.

## Physical premiums

- Physical metal trades at LME + premium: e.g., Rotterdam duty-paid aluminium, US Midwest aluminium premium (CME), Yangshan copper premium (Chinese import demand), CIF Rotterdam nickel.
- Premiums often signal regional tightness before exchange prices do.

## SHFE and arbitrage

- SHFE contracts are in yuan, VAT-inclusive (13%). The **import arbitrage** (SHFE vs LME adjusted for FX, VAT and duties) determines whether China pulls metal in.
- Chinese market hours overnight (in the Western time zone) often set the tone.

## 2022 nickel squeeze lesson

On 8 March 2022, LME nickel rose ~250% in two days due to a large short position (Tsingshan) and the Russia shock; the LME cancelled trades and suspended trading. It led to daily price limits and greater position transparency. **Lesson:** exchange rules can change mid-crisis; size positions for liquidity, not just price.
