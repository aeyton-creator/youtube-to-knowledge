# Iron Ore & Steel

The largest seaborne dry-bulk trade (~1.6 Bt/yr seaborne iron ore). Pricing is effectively a bet on Chinese steel production.

## Supply

- **Australia (~55% of seaborne)**: Rio Tinto, BHP, Fortescue in the Pilbara — lowest-cost producers.
- **Brazil (~25%)**: Vale; higher-grade (65% Fe) Carajás ore; rainy season (Dec–Apr) cuts shipments.
- **Simandou (Guinea)**: giant high-grade project, first shipments from late 2025 — a major medium-term supply increase.
- Others: South Africa, India, Canada, Ukraine.
- Quarterly production reports from the majors are important supply signals; Q1 is weather-impacted (Pilbara cyclones).

## Demand

- **China makes ~53–55% of world crude steel (~1 Bt/yr)** and imports ~70% of seaborne ore.
- Blast furnace (BF-BOF) steelmaking uses ore + coking coal; electric arc furnaces (EAF) use scrap and DRI. India is the main growth market.
- Chinese steel demand: property (structural decline since 2021), infrastructure, manufacturing, and **steel exports** (record levels, triggering trade defense worldwide).
- Production controls/curbs (environmental, output caps) can suddenly cut ore demand.

## Grade and quality

- **62% Fe** is the benchmark; 65% Fe high-grade (Brazil, Canada) earns a premium that widens when steel margins are high (mills want productivity) and narrows when mills cut costs.
- 58% Fe low-grade discounts widen when margins are good.
- Penalties for alumina, silica, phosphorus.

## Price drivers and indicators

1. Chinese steel margins (rebar/HRC minus ore and coke costs) → mill demand.
2. Chinese port stocks (Mysteel ~45 ports) and mill inventories (days of use).
3. Property starts, local government special bond issuance, infrastructure stimulus.
4. Steel export volumes and trade barriers.
5. Supply: Australian/Brazilian shipment data (weekly), Simandou ramp-up.
6. Freight: Capesize rates (Brazil–China C3, West Australia–China C5).
