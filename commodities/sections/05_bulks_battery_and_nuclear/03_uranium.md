# Uranium

Mostly traded via long-term contracts between utilities and producers; the spot market is thin and dominated by traders, financial vehicles (Sprott Physical Uranium Trust, Yellow Cake plc) and producers.

## Pricing

| Market | Basis |
|---|---|
| Spot U3O8 | UxC / TradeTech; $/lb |
| Long-term price | Monthly indicator; more stable, base-escalated contracts |
| CME UxC U3O8 futures | 250 lb, cash-settled — limited liquidity |
| Conversion (UF6) and enrichment (SWU) | Separate fuel-cycle prices, both tight since 2022 |

## Fuel cycle

Mining (U3O8 "yellowcake") → conversion (UF6) → enrichment (SWU) → fuel fabrication → reactor. Each step is concentrated: **Russia (Rosatom) holds ~40% of global enrichment capacity**; the US banned Russian enriched uranium imports from 2024 (waivers until 2028).

## Supply

- **Kazakhstan (Kazatomprom) ~40%** of mined supply (in-situ recovery); constrained by sulphuric acid availability and a higher mineral extraction tax from 2025.
- **Canada (Cameco, Athabasca Basin)**: McArthur River/Key Lake; Cigar Lake.
- Namibia, Australia, Uzbekistan, Niger (coup 2023, Orano lost control of Somair).
- Secondary supply (stockpiles, underfeeding at enrichment plants) bridges the primary deficit.

## Demand

- ~440 operating reactors consuming ~180–190 Mlb U3O8/yr. Growth: China (largest build pipeline), India, reactor life extensions, restarts (Japan), and new interest from tech companies for data centres; SMRs are a longer-term option.
- Utility contracting cycles: under-contracting since the 2010s creates periodic term-buying waves.
