# Electricity (Power)

Power cannot be stored economically at scale (batteries are changing this at the margin), so it is the most volatile commodity and priced by location and hour.

## Key markets

| Region | Venues / hubs | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| US | PJM West, ERCOT North/Houston, CAISO SP15/NP15, MISO, NYISO, ISO-NE | ICE and CME/Nodal futures; ERCOT is energy-only with scarcity pricing up to $5,000/MWh |
| Germany | EEX German baseload/peakload | Europe's benchmark; heavily renewable-influenced |
| France | EEX | Nuclear availability (EDF) drives winter risk |
| Nordics | Nasdaq / EEX (system price) | Hydro reservoir levels dominate |
| UK | ICE / EPEX | Gas-dominated marginal pricing |

## Concepts

- **Merit order:** generators ranked by marginal cost (renewables ≈ 0, nuclear, coal, CCGT gas, peakers). The marginal unit sets price.
- **Baseload vs peakload** products; on-peak/off-peak in the US (5×16, 2×16, 7×8).
- **Spark spread** = power − heat rate × gas price; **dark spread** = power − coal-equivalent. "Clean" versions subtract carbon cost.
- **Duck curve:** solar depresses midday prices, raising evening ramp prices; negative prices are increasingly common at midday.
- **Capacity markets** (PJM, UK) pay for availability, separate from energy prices.

## Drivers

1. Weather (temperature, wind, solar irradiance, hydrology).
2. Fuel prices (gas, coal) and carbon (EUA, UKA, RGGI, California).
3. Plant outages (nuclear in France, thermal outages in ERCOT).
4. Interconnector flows and transmission congestion (basis between nodes and hubs).
5. Load growth from data centres, electrification and reshoring.
