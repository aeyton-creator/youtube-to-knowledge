# Natural Gas & LNG

Gas is regional by pipeline but increasingly global via LNG. The three benchmarks — **Henry Hub (US)**, **TTF (Europe)** and **JKM (North Asia)** — are linked by LNG shipping arbitrage.

## Contract specifications

| Contract | Venue / ticker | Size | Tick | Settlement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Henry Hub | NYMEX NG | 10,000 MMBtu | $0.001 = $10 | Physical at Henry Hub, Louisiana; expires 3 business days before the first calendar day of the delivery month |
| Dutch TTF | ICE Endex TFM | 1 MW/hour for each hour of delivery month (e.g. 744 MWh in a 31-day month) | €0.005/MWh | Physical at TTF virtual hub |
| UK NBP | ICE M | 1,000 therms/day | 0.01 p/therm | Physical |
| JKM LNG | CME / ICE (Platts JKM) | 10,000 MMBtu | $0.001 | Cash-settled |

Conversions: **1 MWh ≈ 3.412 MMBtu**; 1 therm = 0.1 MMBtu; 1 Bcf ≈ 1.037 million MMBtu (≈1 Tbtu); 1 tonne LNG ≈ 52 MMBtu ≈ 1,360 m³ gas.

## US gas (Henry Hub)

- US produces ~100+ Bcf/d. Associated gas from the Permian (tied to oil drilling) and dry gas from Appalachia (Marcellus/Utica) and Haynesville (Louisiana; most price-elastic basin, close to LNG terminals).
- Demand: power (~35–40%), residential/commercial heating, industrial, LNG feedgas (rapid growth: Plaquemines, Corpus Christi Stage 3, Golden Pass), and pipeline exports to Mexico.
- **Storage:** injection season April–October, withdrawal November–March. The weekly EIA storage number vs consensus and the 5-year average is the key data point. End-October storage (~3.8–4.0 Tcf typical) and end-March storage are the critical balance points.
- **Weather is king:** heating/cooling degree days (HDD/CDD) from GFS and ECMWF model runs move prices intraday.
- **Permian bottlenecks:** Waha hub can price negative when pipelines are full; new takeaway pipes change this.
- **Widowmaker:** the March–April (H/J) spread — the bet on end-of-winter storage — is notoriously volatile.
- Data-center power demand is a newer structural growth narrative.

## European gas (TTF)

- Post-2022 Europe replaced most Russian pipeline gas with LNG (US now the largest supplier) and Norwegian pipeline gas.
- **Storage regulation:** EU storage-filling targets (90% by 1 Nov historically, with added flexibility from 2025) drive summer buying; the summer–winter spread determines storage economics.
- Key supply risks: Norwegian maintenance/outages, LNG competition with Asia, end of Russian transit via Ukraine (expired end-2024), EU plan to phase out remaining Russian LNG.
- Demand: heating, power (gas sets the marginal power price in much of Europe; the clean spark spread vs coal and carbon), industry (demand destruction since 2022).

## LNG

- Supply leaders: US, Qatar, Australia; growth wave 2025–2028 from the US and Qatar North Field expansion → widely expected to loosen the market.
- **Arbitrage:** US cargo goes to Asia vs Europe depending on JKM–TTF spread vs extra shipping cost (Panama/Suez/Cape routing).
- US export netback ≈ destination price − shipping − regas − liquefaction tolling fee (~115% × HH + fixed fee for US contracts). When netbacks fall below variable cost, US cargoes get cancelled (as in 2020).
- Oil-indexed long-term contracts in Asia (slope ~11–13% of Brent) compete with spot hub pricing.

## Key drivers

1. Weather (temperature, wind/solar output, hydro levels).
2. Storage vs seasonal norms.
3. LNG outages (e.g., Freeport, Hammerfest, Gorgon) and new trains starting.
4. Geopolitics (Russia, Middle East — Qatar exports pass through Hormuz).
5. Coal-to-gas switching economics and carbon price (Europe).
