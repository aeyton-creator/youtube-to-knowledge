# Soybean Complex (Soybeans, Meal, Oil)

Soybeans are crushed into **meal** (protein for animal feed) and **oil** (food and biofuels). The complex is driven by Brazil supply, Chinese import demand, and increasingly US biofuel policy.

1 bu soybeans = 60 lb. A bushel crushed yields ~**11 lb oil** and ~**44 lb (0.022 st) meal** (48% protein), plus hulls.

## Board crush margin

> **Crush ($/bu) = (Meal $/st × 0.022) + (Oil ¢/lb × 0.11) − Beans $/bu.** Oil share of crush value (oil value ÷ total product value) has risen structurally from ~30–35% to ~40–50% due to renewable diesel demand.

## Supply

| Producer | Share | Calendar |
|---|---|---|
| **Brazil** | ~40%, largest exporter (~100+ Mt exports) | Plant Sep–Dec, harvest Jan–Apr (Mato Grosso first) |
| United States | ~28% | Plant May–Jun, pod-fill **August**, harvest Sep–Nov |
| Argentina | ~12–14% | Largest **meal and oil** exporter (crushes domestically); export taxes (retenciones) a key policy variable |
| Paraguay, China, India, Canada | smaller | |

## Demand

- **China imports ~100–110 Mt of soybeans/yr** (~60% of world trade) to crush for hog and poultry feed. US–China trade relations shift flows between US and Brazil (in tariff disputes, China buys almost exclusively from Brazil).
- Meal demand follows livestock numbers (China hogs, EU, Southeast Asia aquaculture).
- **Soybean oil** demand: cooking, food, and **biofuels** — US renewable diesel/SAF under RFS (RVOs), 45Z clean fuel credit, California LCFS; Brazil and Argentina/Indonesia biodiesel mandates.

## Price drivers

1. Brazilian crop size (CONAB, weather in Mato Grosso, Rio Grande do Sul) and BRL.
2. US August weather; USDA June Acreage and August/September yields.
3. Chinese purchases (USDA flash sales) and US–China trade policy.
4. US biofuel policy (EPA RVO announcements, small refinery exemptions) → soyoil.
5. Argentine export tax changes and farmer selling.
6. Palm oil (substitute veg oil) and crude oil/heating oil (BOHO spread = soyoil vs heating oil — biofuel economics).

## Trading notes

- **Oil share** and **meal–oil spread** trades reflect biofuel demand vs feed demand.
- Brazil premiums (Paranaguá FOB basis) vs US Gulf basis show export competition.
- Jul/Nov old-crop/new-crop and Nov soy/Dec corn ratio for acreage.
