# Wheat

The most widely traded food grain (~800 Mt produced; ~210–220 Mt traded). Unlike corn, global supply is diversified across both hemispheres, and **Black Sea exporters** dominate.

## Classes

- **HRW**: bread wheat, Great Plains (Kansas, Oklahoma, Texas); winter crop planted Sep–Oct, harvested Jun–Jul.
- **SRW**: crackers, cookies; eastern Corn Belt; most liquid but lowest-protein contract.
- **HRS**: high protein (13–14%); Northern Plains and Canada; planted spring, harvested Aug–Sep.
- **Durum**: pasta; Canada, EU, North Africa.
- Protein spreads (KC–Chicago, Minneapolis–KC) widen in years of low protein supply.

## Price drivers

1. Black Sea crop size, Russian export policy and geopolitics.
2. Northern Hemisphere weather: US Plains drought, EU/Russia spring dryness, winterkill, harvest rains (quality).
3. Southern Hemisphere: Australia and Argentina (ENSO-driven).
4. Global stocks — note China holds over half of world wheat stocks, which are not available to the market; use "major exporters' stocks-to-use".
5. Corn–wheat spread (feed substitution).
6. USD and EUR/USD (US vs EU competitiveness).

## Trading notes

- US wheat often trades as the "market of last resort" — the US gains exports only when Black Sea supplies run short.
- Managed money is frequently structurally short Chicago wheat; short-covering rallies on weather/geopolitical headlines are violent.
- Euronext–CBOT spread reflects EU vs US competitiveness after FX.
