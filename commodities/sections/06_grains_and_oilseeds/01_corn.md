# Corn (Maize)

The largest grain crop by volume (~1.2 Bt/yr globally). The US is the largest producer and exporter, but Brazil's second-crop (safrinha) corn has made it a year-round competitor.

## Balance sheet

- **US:** ~90 million acres planted, trend yield ~180+ bu/acre; production ~15+ billion bu.
- Use: **feed & residual (~38–40%)**, **ethanol (~35%)**, exports (~15–20%), food/seed/industrial.
- **Stocks-to-use** ratio is the key price metric; below ~10% is tight, above ~15% is comfortable.
- Major exporters: US, Brazil, Argentina, Ukraine. Importers: Mexico (#1 for US corn), Japan, China (volatile), South Korea, EU, Colombia, Vietnam.

## Price drivers

1. **US weather** June–August (ridge-of-high-pressure patterns, heat during pollination). The "weather premium" is usually built into Dec futures by early summer and removed if conditions are good ("buy the rumour, sell by 4th of July").
2. USDA reports: March Prospective Plantings, June 30 Acreage & Grain Stocks, monthly WASDE, August crop production (first survey-based yield), January Final.
3. Ethanol: EIA weekly production, gasoline demand, ethanol margins, RFS policy (RVOs), E15 waivers.
4. Export competitiveness vs Brazil (FOB Paranaguá vs FOB Gulf) and the BRL.
5. Chinese buying.
6. Corn–soy acreage battle: the **Nov soy / Dec corn price ratio** (above ~2.3–2.5 favours soy; below favours corn).
7. Input costs (nitrogen fertiliser, natural gas).

## Spreads and trading notes

- **Old-crop/new-crop (July/Dec)** spread reflects ending-stocks tightness.
- Corn–wheat spread: wheat feeds when it is cheap relative to corn.
- Basis: Gulf CIF barge, interior elevators; Mississippi River low water (autumn) widens basis and raises barge freight.
- Seasonal: harvest lows Oct–early Nov are common; post-harvest carry rally.
